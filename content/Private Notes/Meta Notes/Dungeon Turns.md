# The Dungeon Turn: The Heartbeat of Adventure

## What is it?

In combat, D&D is precise: 1 Round = 6 Seconds. But during exploration, time often becomes "mushy." Players might spend an hour arguing over a door, or search a room twenty times with no consequence.

The **Dungeon Turn** fixes this. It creates a structured "Exploration Phase" where **Time = Risk.**

- **The Unit:** 1 Dungeon Turn = **10 Minutes** of in-game time.
- **The Cost:** Significant actions cost 1 Turn.
- **The Risk:** At the end of every Turn, the DM checks for **Complications**.

---

## Part 1: How it Works (The Loop)

Just like a Combat Round, a Dungeon Turn follows a procedure:

1. **Declaration:** The players state their goal. _"I want to search the library for the secret map."_
2. **The Transaction:** The DM confirms the cost. _"That requires a thorough search. It will take a Dungeon Turn. Do you proceed?"_
3. **Resolution:** The players roll their checks (Investigation, Thieves' Tools, etc.).
4. **The Clock Ticks:** The DM marks off 1 Turn.
    - Spells with 10-minute or 1-hour durations tick down.
    - Torches/Lanterns burn fuel.
5. **The Check:** The DM rolls the **Complication Die** (usually a d6).
    - **Roll 1:** A Complication occurs immediately.
    - **Roll 2-6:** The dungeon remains quiet.

---

## Part 2: Integrating with the 5-Room Dungeon

The Dungeon Turn prevents the 5-Room Dungeon from becoming a linear "theme park ride." It adds pressure to every room.

### Room 1: The Guardian (The Entry Tax)

Use Turns to measure how "quietly" they enter.

- **Integration:** If they defeat the Guardian quickly (1 Turn), the dungeon is unaware of them. If they spend 3 Turns fighting or debating, the "Alert Level" rises for Room 2.

### Room 2: The Puzzle/RP (The Resource Sink)

This is where Dungeon Turns shine. It stops players from "spamming" dice rolls.

- **Integration:**
    - _The Puzzle:_ "You can try to decipher the runes again (Arcana check), but it will cost another Turn."
    - _The Social:_ "The guard is getting impatient. You have one more Turn of conversation before he asks you to leave."

### Room 3: The Setback (The Time Debt)

The "Setback" often creates a need to backtrack or rush.

- **Integration:** If the Setback is "The ceiling is lowering," switch from 10-minute turns to 1-minute turns (or Combat Rounds) to spike the tension.
- **The Accumulator:** Count how many Turns the party wasted in Rooms 1 and 2. This total creates the "Time Debt" that triggers the Setback. (e.g., _"Because you took 10 Turns to get here, the prisoner has already been moved."_)

### Room 4: The Climax (The Consequence)

The difficulty of the boss fight should be determined by how time was managed in previous rooms.

- **Fast (Few Turns):** The Boss is unbuffed, surprised, or alone.
- **Slow (Many Turns):** The Boss has cast defensive spells, summoned reinforcements, or completed the ritual.

---

## Part 3: Writing Complication Tables

A "Complication" does not always mean "Combat." If you roll a random monster every 10 minutes, the game grinds to a halt. A good table mixes tension, resource drain, and combat.

**The "D6" Standard Table:**

When you roll a **1** on the Complication Die, roll on this sub-table (or pick one that fits):

1. **Immediate Danger (Combat):** A patrolling monster attacks.
2. **Resource Drain:** A torch burns out, a potion leaks, or a spell effect ends early due to anti-magic fluctuations.
3. **Environmental Hazard:** A cave-in blocks the path (requires time to clear), gas leaks (Con saves), or lights go out.
4. **Sensory Spook (Tension):** They hear footsteps, smell rot, or find a fresh corpse. No damage, but it makes them paranoid.
5. **Barriers:** A door that was open is now locked. A guard has moved to block their exit.
6. **Roleplay Complication:** Two PCs get into an argument (due to stress), giving Disadvantage on the next Social check.


---

## Part 4: Tuning the Dial (Frequency & Intensity)

You can adjust the "difficulty setting" of your dungeon by changing the die you use or the threshold for failure.

### 1. Adjusting Frequency (The Die Size)

How often do bad things happen?

- **Hardcore / High Traffic (The d4):** Roll a d4. Complication on a **1**. (25% chance per turn). _Use for: Active enemy bases, collapsing ruins._
- **Standard (The d6):** Roll a d6. Complication on a **1**. (~16% chance per turn). _Use for: Most dungeons._
- **Slow / Empty (The d10):** Roll a d10. Complication on a **1**. (10% chance per turn). _Use for: Ancient tombs, wilderness exploration._


### 2. Adjusting Intensity (The Tension Pool)

Instead of rolling immediately, use a **Tension Pool** (a visible bowl on the table).

- **The Mechanic:** Every time a Turn passes, drop a d6 into the bowl. Do not roll yet.
- **The Trigger:** When a player makes a noise, fails a check, or triggers a trap, **roll ALL dice in the bowl.**
- **The Result:** If _any_ die shows a '1', the Complication happens, and the bowl is emptied.
- **Why use this?** It builds dread. Seeing 1 die in the bowl is fine. Seeing 5 dice in the bowl makes players terrified to open the next door.


---

## Summary for DMs

When designing your 5-Room Dungeon with Dungeon Turns, define three things:

1. **What costs a Turn?** (Usually: Searching, Picking Locks, Rituals, Short Rests).
2. **What is the Risk?** (Create a 6-item Complication Table specific to the dungeon's theme).
3. **What is the Consequence?** (Decide how the _Total Time Taken_ affects the Boss in Room 4).
