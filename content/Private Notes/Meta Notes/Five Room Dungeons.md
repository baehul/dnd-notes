# The 5-Room Dungeon: The Universal Pacing Engine

## What is it?

The **5-Room Dungeon** is a design framework, not a strict architectural blueprint. It is a method for organizing game sessions to ensure **pacing**, **variety**, and **dramatic tension**.

While it is called a "dungeon," it applies to any adventure site: a haunted forest, a political banquet, a sci-fi heist, or a literal stone crypt.

The philosophy creates a complete narrative arc in a single session:

**Introduction $\rightarrow$ Challenge $\rightarrow$ Rising Tension $\rightarrow$ Climax $\rightarrow$ Resolution.**

---
## Part 1: The Structure (The 5 Rooms)

### Room 1: The Entrance Guardian

**The Barrier to Entry.**

This room establishes the theme and proves the players are worthy of the adventure. It prevents the players from simply walking to the treasure.

- **Purpose:** To transition players from the "safe world" to the "adventure world."
- **Types:**
    - **Combat:** A group of goblins guarding the cave mouth.
    - **Skill:** A cliff face that must be climbed or a magical portal that requires an Arcana check to open.
    - **Social:** A receptionist at a corporate HQ who demands an appointment.

### Room 2: The Puzzle or Roleplay Challenge

**The Change of Pace.**

If Room 1 was combat, Room 2 should be quiet. If Room 1 was a skill check, Room 2 should be interactive. This room creates variety and allows non-combat characters to shine.

- **Purpose:** To prevent "combat fatigue" and engage the players' minds.
- **Types:**
    - **Puzzle:** A riddle door, a floor-tile pattern, or deciphering a code.
    - **Roleplay:** Negotiating with a prisoner, calming a spooked animal, or bluffing past a servant.
    - **Exploration:** Navigating a trap-filled hallway or a maze.

### Room 3: The Trick or Setback

**The Narrative Twist.**

Just as the players feel they are making progress, something goes wrong. This is crucial for building tension before the finale. It costs resources (HP, spells, time) and raises the stakes.

- **Purpose:** To add dramatic irony and prevent the adventure from feeling like a linear "to-do list."
- **Types:**
    - **The Trick:** The "treasure" is fake; the prisoner is actually a doppelganger.
    - **The Setback:** The floor collapses, separating the party; the alarm is triggered; the rival adventuring party arrives first.
    - **The Choice:** To proceed, they must sacrifice something valuable.

### Room 4: The Climax

**The Big Show.**

This is the reason the players came here. It is the final test of their resources and tactics.

- **Purpose:** To provide a satisfying resolution to the conflict.
- **Types:**
    - **The Boss Fight:** A high-CR monster with minions.
    - **The Race:** Escaping the collapsing dungeon or stopping the ritual before the timer runs out.
    - **The Debate:** A high-stakes social trial where the players' lives hang on a Persuasion roll.

### Room 5: The Reward and Revelation

**The Decompression.**

The tension breaks. The players receive their prize, but the story isn't over.

- **Purpose:** To reward effort and pivot the story toward the _next_ adventure.
- **Types:**
    - **The Loot:** Gold, magic items, titles.
    - **The Revelation:** Finding a letter on the villain’s body that points to a bigger threat ("The Hook").
    - **The Change:** The prisoners are free, or the curse is lifted from the land.

## Part 2: Modifying and Breaking the Rules

The "Rooms" are metaphorical scenes. You can bend the geometry to fit your needs.

### 1. The Non-Linear Dungeon

You do not have to arrange them 1-2-3-4-5.

- **The Hub & Spoke:** Room 1 is a central hub. Rooms 2, 3, and 4 are separate hallways branching off it. You must clear 2 and 3 to unlock 4.
- **The Parallel Path:** Room 2 and Room 3 are side-by-side options. Players choose: "Do we take the trapped hallway (Room 2) or the guarded barracks (Room 3)?"

### 2. The Narrative "Room"

A "Room" can be a concept.

- _Example:_ A murder mystery in a mansion.
    - **Room 1:** The Body is found (The Guardian/Hook).
    - **Room 2:** Interrogating suspects (Roleplay).
    - **Room 3:** Another murder happens _while_ the players are investigating (The Setback).
    - **Room 4:** Confronting the Killer in the library (Climax).
    - **Room 5:** The reading of the will (Reward).

## Part 3: Scaling the Framework (Fractal Design)

The true power of the 5-Room Dungeon is that it is **fractal**. You can use it for a 2-hour session, or a 2-year campaign.

### 1. The "One-Shot" (Micro Scale)

This is the standard use. A single location (cave, tower, ship) that contains 5 encounters.

- _Time to play:_ 3 to 4 hours.

### 2. The "Megadungeon" (Mid Scale)

Instead of building a 100-room sprawling maze that bores players, build **five 5-Room Dungeons** and link them together.

- **The Dungeon Chain:**
    - **Level 1 (The Upper Ruins):** A 5-Room dungeon themed around bandits. Room 5 contains the key to the Crypts below.
    - **Level 2 (The Crypts):** A 5-Room dungeon themed around undead. Room 5 contains the location of the deep caves.
    - **Level 3 (The Deep Caves):** A 5-Room dungeon themed around aberrations.

### 3. The "Campaign Arc" (Macro Scale)

You can structure an entire Level 1-20 campaign using the 5-Room pacing.

- **"Room" 1 (Levels 1-4):** The Local Hero Arc. The players protect their town (The Entrance Guardian to the world).
- **"Room" 2 (Levels 5-9):** The Political Arc. Players deal with kings and spies (Roleplay/Puzzle challenge).
- **"Room" 3 (Levels 10-14):** The Disaster. The Kingdom falls, the villain wins, or the world breaks (The Setback).
- **"Room" 4 (Levels 15-19):** The War. The players rally the survivors to storm the villain's citadel (The Climax).
- **"Room" 5 (Level 20):** Ascension. The players become gods or legends (The Reward).

## Summary Checklist for DMs

When designing your session, ask:

1. **Is there a clear barrier to entry?** (Guardian)
2. **Is there a moment where swords are useless?** (Puzzle/RP)
3. **Is there a moment where things go wrong?** (Setback)
4. **Is there a clear main target?** (Climax)
5. **Does the reward point to the next session?** (Revelation)
