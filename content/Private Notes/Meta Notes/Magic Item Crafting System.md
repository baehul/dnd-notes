
In this campaign, crafting magic items is a ritualistic process that goes beyond simple gold costs. It requires a combination of specific monster parts ("Hard Components") and abstract magical energy ("Soft Components").

## Core Components

### 1. The Key Component (Hard Requirement)
Every magic item requires a unique physical anchor, usually harvested from a monster or found during an adventure. This ties the item to the narrative.
* *Example:* "The intact eye of a Brown Scavver" for a Rope Trick Room.
* *Example:* "The blood of a creature that can manipulate darkness" for a Seadevil's Shield.

### 2. Magic Levels (Soft Requirement)
Instead of just expending a spell slot, the crafter must imbue the item with a specific volume of magical energy, measured in "Levels."
* *Definition:* A "Level" corresponds to spell slot levels (e.g., casting a 3rd-level spell contributes 3 Levels).
* *Schools:* The magic must match the item's function (e.g., Abjuration for protection, Transmutation for physical alteration).
* *Example:* "11 levels of abjuration magic."
* *Example:* "12 levels of transmutation magic and 6 levels of conjuration."

### 3. Thematic Reagents & Gold Cost
Gold is not just melted down; it is spent on specific, thematic materials required for the ritual.
* *Example:* "300 gold worth of tiger's eye gem dust."
* *Example:* "200 gold worth of magical chalk."
* *Example:* "A bucket of seawater" or "A drop of octopus ink."

### 4. The Ritual Process
Crafting is a step-by-step narrative action, not a passive downtime activity. The player must describe *how* they combine the materials.
* *Example:* "Draw the outline of the Room in 3D using the eye as the drawing utensil."
