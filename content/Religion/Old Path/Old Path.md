**The Old Path** is an ancient, animistic belief system and collection of oral traditions that predate [[The Shattering of the World]]. Unlike the organized, theocratic structure of the [[Ecclesia Solari]] or the philosophical skepticism of the [[Way Of Night (Religion)|Way Of Night]], The Old Path is a decentralized folk religion focused on the preservation of cultural memory. Its central tenet is the remembrance of the world as it existed before [[The Shattering of the World|The Shattering]].
# Adherents and Demographics
The faith is primarily observed by the [[Ancient Ones]], many of whom are old enough to possess personal memories of the pre-Shattering era. For these dragons, giants, and ancient spirits, The Old Path is not a matter of faith, but of history. Within the [[Blood Horde]], The Old Path exists as a secondary spiritual practice. While the majority of the tribes revere the **[[Blood Gods]]** (ancestral and totemic spirits of war and vengeance), village elders and storytellers often preserve the fables of The Old Path as a way to explain the brSoken nature of the world to the younger generations.
# Foundational Myths
The mythology of The Old Path is distinct for its focus on physical phenomena that are now impossible in the [[Strata]]. 
## The Hare and The Stars
Long ago, before the world broke, Hare looked up and saw those same stars. He fell in love with their cold, bright beauty. "I will marry them," Hare said. "I will catch them."

Hare was fast. He was faster than the wolf and faster than the wind. He thought, "If I run to the edge of the Earth, I can jump off and catch my bride." So he ran. He ran until his paws bled. He ran until his brown fur turned the grey of old age. He ran, chasing the horizon, expecting to find a cliff. But after years of running, Hare stumbled into a clearing and stopped. He saw a burrow - his burrow. He had run in a straight line and ended up exactly where he started.

Hare wept and beat his paws on the ground. "Mother Earth!" he cried. 'Where is the end? Where is the cliff so I may jump to my love?'" And Earth laughed, a deep rumble that shook the trees. 

"Silly Hare," she whispered. "I have no beginning and I have no end. You are from me and you cannot leave me."

But Hare never gave up. That is why, even today, the Hare does not run smooth like the wolf. He hops. He leaps. He jumps high into the air, hoping that this time, he will finally reach the stars.
## The River's Long Walk
In the First Days, River was born in the snowy lap of her mother, Mountain. River was young and loud. "I want to see the world!'" she roared. Mountain sighed, her stone shoulders heavy. "We are rock and ice, daughter. We stay. If you leave, you will be lost."

"I will not get lost," River promised. "I will come back." So River ran. 

She tumbled down the slopes, laughing. She met her sisters, Creek and Stream, and they held hands, growing wider and stronger. They ran through the forests and the cities of old. Finally, they tasted salt. They saw Grandmother Ocean waiting with open arms.

River crashed into Ocean’s embrace and danced for many days. But soon, the salt stung her tongue. She missed the cool stone of her mother. "Grandmother," River asked, "Send me home.'"

"I cannot," rumbled Ocean. "Only Sun can carry you now."

River looked up, terrified. But Sun reached down with warm, invisible hands. He lifted River up, lighter than breath, turning her into Cloud. She floated back high in the sky, seeing all the world below, until she saw Mountain again. She let go, falling as Rain, kissing her mother’s face.

"You returned!" Mountain cried. "Will you stay now? Will you be stone like me?"

River remembered running through the forests and cities with her sisters. She remembered the taste of the salt and the dance of the waves. She remembered Sun's warm embrace and flying through the sky.

"No, mother," she said. "I think I will be a River." And she ran down the slope again.
## The Sun and Moon
Once, Sun and Moon lived in the same sky, but they had never met. Sun was loud and burned with gold fire. Moon was quiet, shy, and wore a dress of shimmering silver silk.

One day, Sun looked across the sky and saw Moon. He fell in love instantly. '"You are beautiful!" he shouted, and he ran toward her to hold her. But Sun did not know his own strength. As he stepped close, his heat began to scorch Moon’s beautiful silver hem.

Moon was frightened. She ran, slipping behind the curve of the world.

Sun laughed. "A game!" he roared. He chased her. He ran across the sky, painting the blue day with his trail, looking for her under every rock. But Moon was clever. She waited until Sun was tired and went to sleep beneath the world. Only then did she creep out, painting the sky black so she could move unseen.

To this day, they run. When Sun rises, Moon hides. And when Moon must walk the sky, she is careful. Sometimes she shows her whole silver dress. But when Sun gets too close, she covers herself in shadow, hiding her face, until he passes her by

## The Four Robes of Earth
Long ago, Earth was always grey. She was sad, for she felt plain. She went to Father Time and begged, "Make me beautiful."

Father Time nodded. 'I will weave you four robes,' he said. 'But you must change them when I say. You cannot wear the same gown forever.'

First, he gave her the Green Robe. It smelled of rain and mud. When Earth put it on, the flowers bloomed and the animals woke up to dance on her skirts. This was Spring.

Next, he gave her the Gold Robe. It was heavy and warm, stitched with wheat and sunlight. When Earth wore this, Sun stayed long in the sky to stare at her beauty. This was Summer.

Then, Father Time gave her the Red Robe of Autumn. It was the color of dying embers. When Earth put it on, the wind grew cold, and her beautiful green hair turned brown and fell out. The animals stopped dancing. Earth cried, "I am ugly again! Give me back the Gold!'"

"Not yet," said Father Time. He wrapped her in the White Robe. It was made of thick wool and ice. It was heavy, so heavy that Earth could not dance. She could not speak. She fell into a deep, deep sleep, all through Winter.

But when she woke up? She was rested. She was strong. And waiting for her was the Green Robe, ready to be worn again.

## The First Tree
In the beginning, there was only Rock and Dust. Then, from a single seed dropped by the stars, came the First Tree.

The First Tree was tall; taller than the mountains. But she was lonely. She looked at her shadow and saw nothing moving. So, she shook her branches. Her leaves fell and became the Beetles. Her bark fell and became the Mice. Her acorns fell and became the Wolves. From her roots, grew the Grass.

"Go, my children," said the First Tree. "Run and fly. Take root and grow."

But the animals were afraid. '"Mother," they asked, "What if we get hungry? What if we get tired? What if we die?"

The First Tree smiled, rustling her leaves. "Do not fear," she said. "We are all one. When you are hungry, eat my fruit. When you are tired, sleep in my shade. And when you die? You will lie down at my feet, and your bodies will become the soil that feeds my roots. I will drink you in, and you will become new fruit, new shade, and new life."

The plants were afraid too. "Mother," they asked, "What if we get hungry? What if we get dry? What if we get eaten?"

The First Tree smiled, once again. "Do not fear," she said. "We are all one. When you are hungry, you will eat the same soil that feeds my roots. When you get dry, my roots will share their water with you. And when you get eaten? Your bodies will nourish the animals who become the soil and water that feeds your roots. You will be eaten, and you will become new soil, new water, and new life."

And so the Great Circle began. The Grass fed the Beetles. The Beetles fed the Mice who fed the Wolves and the Wolves fed the Grass.
