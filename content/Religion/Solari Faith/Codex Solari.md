The **Codex Solari** is the sacred scripture of the [[Solaris and The Solari Faith|Solari Faith]] and the doctrinal foundation of the [[Ecclesia Solari]]. The faithful hold it to be the word of [[Solaris and The Solari Faith|Solaris]] himself, and it speaks in his own voice. Its verses supply the words of the daily prayers, the laws by which the Church judges the faithful and the unfaithful, and the doctrine of death and damnation for which the faith is best known.

The Codex's central thesis is the opposite of the [[Way Of Night (Book)|Way Of Night]]'s: it admits no doubt. Solaris is presented as absolute, his law as fixed, and his judgment as immediate. As the Benevolent Father, he promises warmth, growth and protection to those who are obedient and devout. As the Scouring Fire, he answers any breach of his law at once and without appeal.

# History
The origins of the Codex Solari are known only through the faith's own teaching. The [[Ecclesia Solari]] holds that the Codex was given by [[Solaris and The Solari Faith|Solaris]] rather than composed, and it names no author, compiler or first copyist. It teaches that the text has passed down whole and unaltered in the Church's keeping, and it keeps the copies few: by the Codex's own law, a copy may be held only by a priest or by the Church itself. No copy is lawfully held outside the Church's custody, and the Church admits no rival record against which its account could be tested. In this the Codex stands apart from the [[Way Of Night (Book)|Way Of Night]], whose many divergent manuscripts are a matter of open study.

Knowledge of the Codex has spread with the Church. The Church's priests and monks keep the faith's records, conduct its education and teach the Codex to the faithful, and the religious education the Church requires has carried its teaching into the households of the [[Upper Crust Houses|Upper Houses]]. The [[Golden Sails Trading & Shipping Company|Golden Sails Company]] is bound, in return for the Church's services, to spread the word of Solaris along its shipping routes, and it has carried the faith across the world. Authority over the meaning of the text rests with the Church, led by [[Pontifex Maximus Virtue|the Pontifex Maximus]], the Voice of Solaris on the Shattered World. Even so, both of the Church's partners in [[The Crusade]] have drawn their own doctrines from the Codex. The Church counts the Houses' reading a heresy, yet it has tolerated both readings, as it tolerates the Houses and the Company themselves, as necessary to its mission.

# Composition and Structure
The Codex is divided into three books named for the three stations of the sun, which are also the three hours of daily prayer: the Book of Dawn, the Book of Zenith and the Book of Dusk. Each book contains twenty-seven chapters. Each chapter is built of verses grouped in threes, so every passage closes on a verse divisible by three. Passages are cited by book, chapter and verse: *Dusk 3:9*, for example, is the ninth verse of the third chapter of the Book of Dusk.

The arrangement reflects [[The Universal Constant|the Law Of Threes]], and Solari teachers point to its powers of three as a sign of the text's divine stability. The [[Way Of Night (Religion)|Way Of Night]]'s triadic meter is likewise cited as a source of the number's importance across the world. The Church does not entertain the comparison.

- **The Book of Dawn** concerns the covenant between Solaris and the faithful: his return each morning, the opening of the eye of the world, and the terms on which he offers warmth, growth and protection to those who keep his law. It contains the Greeting, the Covenant that names him Father to the obedient and Fire to the lawbreaker, the law of the keeping of the word, and the verses on gold.
- **The Book of Zenith** concerns law and judgment, and takes its name from the hour when shadows are smallest and nothing is hidden. It contains the Exaltation, the verses on rule, a prophecy of judgment upon a mingled world, the purging of the Unclean, and the condemnation of the Idols of Shadow. It closes with the Breaches, which name the chief offences against the law and the penalty for each.
- **The Book of Dusk** concerns the night, which the faith regards as a time of spiritual siege, and death. It contains the Gratitude, the law of the hearth and the night watch together with the words of the ash-marking, and the rites of cremation and burial that divide the faithful dead, who rise on the smoke, from the buried, who wander the cold dark.

# Textual Authority
Unlike the [[Way Of Night (Book)|Way Of Night]], the Codex has a single authoritative text, and copies of it are few. The [[Ecclesia Solari|Church]] teaches that no word of it has been added or taken away. Only a priest or the Church itself may hold a copy; for anyone else, possessing one is itself a breach of the law, and the copy is taken back into the Church's keeping, and the one who kept it wears the ash. The Church holds that a copy bearing any word not found in the Codex is Unclean, whoever holds it, and belongs in the brazier. Keeping, copying and teaching the Codex fall to the Church's priests and monks as part of their charge over the faith's records and education. The faithful learn its passages by hearing them taught and recited rather than from the page. The Church alone holds the authority to say what a verse means.

That authority is not always unchallenged. Two passages in particular are read differently outside the Church:
- **Dawn 7:1–3**, on gold, underpins the [[Golden Sails Trading & Shipping Company|Golden Sails Company]]'s doctrine that gold is sunlight given form and belongs to the children of the Sun. The Church reads the same verses as a command to consecrate gathered wealth to Solaris.
- **Zenith 2:1–3**, on rule, is cited by the Church as proof that only Solaris grants authority. The [[Upper Crust Houses]] read it as Solaris's own testimony that he set their bloodlines in their place.

# Use in Worship
The words of the three daily prayers come from the first chapter of each book: the Greeting from Dawn 1, the Exaltation from Zenith 1 and the Gratitude from Dusk 1. The congregation answers each prayer with a fixed response. The faithful, including the children of the noble Houses, learn these passages by heart through the Church's mandatory religious education.

# Commonly Cited Passages
The following passages are among the most often recited and cited, grouped by theme.

## The Daily Prayers
**The Greeting** (Dawn 1:1–3)
> Face the east, for I return.  
> The eye of the world is opened, and nothing is hidden from it.  
> My fire has kept your door through the night, and now I keep it. Rise, and be seen.  

*Response: "Welcome, Father. The eye is open. We are seen, and we are glad."*

**The Exaltation** (Zenith 1:1–3)
> This is the hour of the smallest shadow.  
> Set down your work and lift up your face, for there is none above me.  
> Bow beneath me: this is your place, and I have given it to you.  

*Response: "You alone are great. We are beneath you, and it is good."*

**The Gratitude** (Dusk 1:1–3)
> I have gone before you the whole of the day, and you were kept.  
> Now I go down, and the night cometh up against you.  
> Keep my fire, and endure until I return.  

*Response: "Thanks for the day. Strength for the night. The fire will not go out."*

## The Hearth, the Vigil and the Ash
**The Vigil** (Dusk 3:1–9)
> When I go down, I leave my fire behind me; it is a piece of me, and it keepeth the door.  
> It shall not go out between my going and my return.  
> A dark house is invited ground, and the wicked shall come into it.  
> The house whose fire dieth in the night hath opened its own door;  
> whatsoever cometh in, that house invited, and I will not come down to drive it out.  
> In the morning that house shall take ash from its cold hearth, and wear it.  
> Let not a house sleep all at once while I am gone;  
> let one feed the fire, and one guard it, and one wait upon the east.  
> Keep watch in threes, and the third watch shall greet me.  

**The Ash** (Dusk 3:10–12)
> By the fire, cleansed.  
> By the ash, marked.  
> By the Sun, seen.  

## Death and Burial
**The Dead** (Dusk 9:1–9)
> When one of my faithful dieth, he shall be given to the fire, and to nothing else.  
> The fire shall loose him from the earth, and he shall rise upon the smoke,  
> and I will take him up into myself.  
> But the criminal, and the heretic, and the enemy of my faithful shall not be burned.  
> They shall be put into the earth and covered over, for the earth is cold, and the cold is theirs.  
> None shall weep over them, for I weep not.  
> The buried man shall not rise, for the smoke cannot reach him;  
> he shall not see my face again, nor feel my warmth;  
> he shall wander the cold dark beneath all things, and I will not look upon him.  

## The Unclean and the Idols of Shadow
**The Purging** (Zenith 7:1–9)
> Whatsoever the rot hath touched shall not be eaten, but shall be given to the brazier.  
> Whatsoever the monstrous hath touched shall not be kept, but shall be given to the brazier.  
> He that asketh whether a thing be clean enough hath answered himself, for it is not.  
> And if a man give himself to the shadow and will not come out of it,  
> none shall pity him, neither hide him, neither plead for him;  
> he shall be given to the fire, that the fire may do for him what he would not do.  
> Whatsoever book speaketh against me, no man shall read it to learn its error,  
> nor keep it for its beauty, nor for its age, nor for the hand that wrote it.  
> Give it to the brazier, and let the fire read it for you.  

**The Idols of Shadow** (Zenith 9:1–3)
> There are things in the dark that call themselves gods; they are not gods, but Idols of Shadow.  
> They are weak, and they feed upon them that listen, and they give nothing but confusion.  
> The blind will tell you that blindness is sight. Answer them with fire.  

## The Covenant and the Breaches
**The Covenant** (Dawn 3:1–9)
> Unto him that keepeth my law I am the Father:  
> I warm his house, and ripen his field, and stand at his door against the dark.  
> This is my covenant, and I will not break it.  
> Unto him that breaketh my law I am the Fire:  
> I tarry not for him, neither do I ask of him twice.  
> This also is my covenant, and I will not break it.  
> Nothing is done beneath my eye that I see not,  
> and there is no shadow so deep that it shall hide a man from me.  
> He that walketh in my light shall be warm; and he that walketh out of it shall burn.  

**The Breaches** (Zenith 27:1–9)
> These are the breaches of my law and the measure of each, spoken at the height of the day, when there is no shadow to hide them.  
> Whoso boweth to an Idol of Shadow shall be cast out from among my faithful in that day.  
> Whoso teacheth another to bow shall be given to the fire in that day.  
> Whoso worketh through the hour of the smallest shadow shall wear the ash, and every eye that seeth him shall know why.  
> Whoso letteth his hearth go dark between my going and my return shall wear the ash likewise.  
> And he that doeth these things again shall be cast out.  
> Whoso putteth my faithful dead into the earth shall himself be put into the earth in his turn.  
> Whoso keepeth the Unclean in his house, his house shall be emptied into the brazier.  
> This is the law of the hour of the smallest shadow: the ash for the small sin, the fire for the great, and the measure shall not be lessened for any man.  

## The Keeping of the Word
**The Keeping of the Word** (Dawn 5:1–3)
> My word shall be kept by my priests and in my house, and in no other; and the copies thereof shall be few, that no hand may change it.  
> Whoso would hear my word, let him come unto my priests, and they shall speak it unto him.  
> He that is not my priest and keepeth my word in his own house hath stolen it; it shall be taken from him, and he shall wear the ash.  

## Prophecy
**The Prophecy of the Mingled World** (Zenith 3:1–3)
> A day shall come when the wicked shall lie down beside the righteous, and the dark shall eat at the hearts of the innocent.  
> In that day I will strike the world in the height of my wrath, and it shall be broken;  
> and I will part them, the one from the other, and they shall not lie down together again.  

## Rule and Wealth (disputed readings)
**On Rule** (Zenith 2:1–3)
> None shall rule beneath me whom I have not set in his place.  
> That which I have set up shall stand, and that which I have not set up I will cast down.  
> Let him that would know who ruleth lift up his eyes.  

**On Gold** (Dawn 7:1–3)
> I poured my light into the deep places of the world, and it grew heavy, and it was gold.  
> It is mine, and I have given it unto my children.  
> Let them gather it up, and let not the shadow keep it.
