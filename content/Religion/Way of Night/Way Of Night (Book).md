The **Way Of Night** is the foundational sacred text of the [[Way Of Night (Religion)|Way Of Night]] religious and philosophical movement. Adherents regard the text as the primary doctrinal guide for approaching life. The text is traditionally believed by its followers to have been directly authored or divinely revealed by [[The Goddess Whose Name Is Night]].

The text is structurally unique, composed of many five stanzas that follow a 3-6-9-6-3 syllable pattern. This triadic meter is cited as a primary cultural catalyst for symbolic importance of the [[number three]] in cultures across the world.

The *Way Of Night*'s central thesis is the rejection of absolute truth and the promotion of intellectual skepticism. This theological stance is reflected in the text's composition: no single authoritative version exists. While adherents frequently compose and add new stanzas to their personal copies, core doctrines and consensus beliefs are established through the frequency of their appearance across the widest range of extant manuscripts.

# Textual Authority and Variability
A defining characteristic of the _Way of Night_ is the lack of a single, authoritative canon. The text's theology encourages the rejection of absolute truth, a principle which extends directly to the text itself. Consequently, adherents traditionally engage in a practice of adding new, individually composed stanzas to their personal copies of the text.

As a result, no two manuscripts are identical. Scholars note that the majority of copies contain numerous stanzas whose origins are unknown or are directly attributed to local scribes and philosophers rather than to [[the Goddess Whose Name Is Night]].
## Commonly accepted stanzas
A few stanzas are widely accepted as originating from the primary manuscript given the proliferation amongst various disparate copies. They are listed below:

> I am Night.  
> I share a truth with you  
> that you have known since The Creation;  
> All truths are empty but this:  
> You must choose.  

>O Great Night,  
>remind that which I know:  
>I have always been and will be blind.  
>To be blind is to see  
>Night is blind.  

>If you are,  
>then you must also be.
>Is Silver silver just for its name?  
>It acts as Silver must,
>so it is.  

>Fear only  
>knowing what is The Light; 
>for this is the rejection of Night.  
>Fear not the being lost.  
>Fear no Night.  

> Am I Night?
> How do you know my voice?
> I live only within what you know.
> You are me, and I, you.
> You are Night.

>The sculptor,  
>has given you their tools.  
>Is mastery recreating her?  
>To become a sculptor,  
>you must sculpt.

> The forest  
> has many paths to find  
> They may split; they all reach the same end.  
> Why then spurn other paths  
> for this one? 

>"Who are you?"  
>cry the Sun, Moon, and stars.  
>"I am Night," you must shout back to them,  
>for you will never know  
>who are you.  

>Draw a star.  
>Would I know which it is?  
>No. It needs all others to be named.  
>Why then must the answer  
>be certain?  

>Consider  
>fish swimming through the sea.  
>For them, air means death. Does that then mean  
>air is the death of you?  
>Are you fish?  

> Night, magic,  
> two faces of the same moon.  
> Is the moon's dark face forever dark,  
> or does it simply wait  
> for a light?  

>You are Night.  
>So are all around you.  
>Do you curse Night for being what it is?  
>First, curse yourself, who tries  
>to know Night.  

>The night sky,  
>its stars uncountable.  
>Why then do you seek to limit them?  
>Is a star beautiful  
>when alone?  

>Do not see.  
>The sight limits the mind.  
>Truth is often found beyond the light,  
>where the eye fails to find  
>any form.  

>A soft word  
>whispered into the wind.  
>It sings in the ear of all who hear;  
>but the source was the wind,  
>not the word.  

>The harvest.  
>You are me, and I, you.  
>The single field gives all blooms a name.  
>Are they distinct or same?  
>They are Night.  

>I am Night.  
>You are me, and I you.  
>You have written these words to yourself,  
>reminding you the truth,  
>there are none.  

>"Night," you plead,  
>"I pray show me the Way."  
>"I am Night." you say back to myself.  
> "I see now. She is Night,  
> as am I."
