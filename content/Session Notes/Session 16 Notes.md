## Summary
### Arrival at the Sinking City
During the remainder of their downtime, [[Cletus Ironplow]] worked on a magic item recipe for an unnamed [[Harmony Engine]] augment. The party's [[Lairs|Lair]] arrived at the [[Sinking City]], a Grade 2 swamp-filled island shaped like a crescent. Rather than the party selecting a docking point, the [[Lairs|Lair]] autonomously moored on the far side of the island, safely away from other ships and traffic. Upon docking, the edge of the party's island physically shifted, becoming wet and marshy to seamlessly match the Sinking City's swampy terrain.

### The Swamp Trek and the Crocodile Ambush
The party began their trek through the dense swamp toward the city proper. [[Twilight Emberrest]] utilized magic to allow the group to walk safely across the surface of the deep mud and water. Along the path, they observed glass bottles hanging from tree branches. These bottles were filled with various trinkets such as coins and beads, alcohol, and liquids like blood or honey. 

Suddenly, the group was ambushed by crocodiles and carnivorous fish. A chaotic skirmish ensued, during which the party accidentally shattered several of the hanging bottles. The party pushed their advantage and killed the crocodiles even as the beasts attempted to retreat. [[Otis Thulu]] sensed distinct displeasure from the World Tree regarding this action, noting that the beasts lacked higher consciousness and were merely retreating in accordance with their nature.

### The Ferryman and the Spirits
Following the battle, a figure poling a raft emerged from the dense fog. Introducing himself as [[The Ferryman]], he offered the party directions and a ride. He explained that the hanging bottles were a local religious custom meant to appease the swamp spirits, who were now angered by the destruction of their offerings. To calm the spirits and make amends, the party was instructed to create new bottle offerings. They successfully did so by sharing precious stories and placing important personal tokens into the new bottles before hanging them from the branches.
